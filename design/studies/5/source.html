Inside PDX

Where every nonstop starts

Three ways to draw the terminal, all built from the same real building outlines and gate positions. Each one ends with the small inset a destination page would carry. Pick a concourse to compare them.

Four concourses, 53 gates. Tap a concourse on any map.

Style one · Diagram

Like a transit map

Concourses straightened into lines, gates as stops in their real order and on their real side. Clear at any size, and it matches the route maps. Least sense of the building itself.

  • Gate order and sides come from the real gate positions; spacing is even.
  • Landmarks sit at the gate they're nearest to.
  • Easiest to keep current when airlines move.
Style two · Plan

The real footprint, drawn flat

The building's true outline from above: the timber roof over the main terminal, carpet down the concourses, jet bridges and parked aircraft at the gates. Realistic and still easy to read.

  • Outlines, gates and places are from OpenStreetMap, to scale.
  • Parked aircraft and trees are illustrative, not live.
  • Works as a big map and shrinks well into an inset.
Style three · Model

A raised model of the building

The same footprint lifted into 3D, seen from the curb side: glass walls, the timber roof, the control tower. The most sense of place, and the hardest to label and keep tidy.

  • Heights are approximate and stretched a little so the shapes read.
  • Gates and places are hard to show without clutter at this angle.
  • Could pair with the plan: model on the Inside PDX page, plan for insets.
